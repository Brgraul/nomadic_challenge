Augsburg Grid Data Primer

The package gives you real power-line locations from OpenStreetMap and a made-up windstorm on top of them. A storm hit, six vague reports came in over two hours, and somewhere in the network there are hidden faults. Your drones have to find them. This page shows what the data contains, what it cannot tell you, and what is worth calculating from it.

What's in the folder

Six files, three kinds of thing

Lines and points are the grid. Reports and areas are the storm. The CSV and GeoJSON files hold the same rows; use CSV in a spreadsheet and GeoJSON in a map tool or code. Every column is explained in the field guide below.

Grid · lines grid_segments.csv / .geojson

64 line sections. Each has two ends (from_node, to_node), a voltage, a length and the full path shape. All are overhead lines.

LINE_024 SUBSTATION_07 → SUBSTATION_01 110 kV · 26.84 km · circuits 1 · cables 3

Useful fields: voltage_kv, voltages_kv, length_km, circuits, cables, geometry_wkt, source_data_quality (what is missing).

Grid · points grid_nodes.csv / .geojson

59 points where lines end or meet. node_type tells you what the point is:

junction 20 several line ends meet boundary_endpoint 19 line cut at map edge abstract_substation 15 substation area intermediate 4 two lines join end to end terminal 1 line just ends

connected_segments lists the lines that touch the point.

Storm · reports initial_information.csv

Six fictional reports: weather alert, a fallen branch, a protection trip, a flash, ticking and warmth, a swinging span. Each has a time (T+01:03), a confidence and a free-text description.

SYN_REPORT_04 visual_flash_report T+01:03 "brief orange flash near a private industrial site"
Storm · areas scenario_context.geojson

The storm footprint polygon plus one box per report. These are the only locations the reports give you.

Storm · settings scenario_public_config.json

The same reports and areas in one JSON file, plus the study box (10.45–10.88 E, 48.09–48.38 N) and the 4-hour window. Easiest file to load in code.

Before you trust it

What the data cannot tell you

Where the faults areThe organisers keep the real fault list. You only get vague reports.
Whether a line is liveA voltage value is what the line is built for, not proof it is energised. Your sensors can only recharge on a live line.
Who gets power from whatNo power flow, no customers. A gap between lines is not an outage.
Inside a substationLines entering the same substation are not proven to connect. The square marks the station area, not the wire ends.
CrossingsTwo lines crossing on the map are not joined. Only exact shared end points count as connections.
Edges of the map19 points are just where the study box cut a line. The line keeps going in reality.
Exact report locationsEach report box is approximate and may be wrong. Several lines sit in every box.
Complete attributesAll 64 lines lack a start date, 60 lack a name, 28 lack an operator and 21 lack a circuit count.
What to calculate

Six numbers that turn the data into a flight plan

Each card names the question, the fields it uses and the answer from this dataset. The last two are interactive: change the weights or the drone specs and the numbers update.

1 · Which lines could each report be about?report polygon × segment geometry

Clip every line to every report box. This turns "somewhere in the east-central area" into a short list of candidate lines and the kilometres you would have to fly.

ReportTimeWhat was seenConfidenceLineskm to check
2 · Where do reports overlap?result of #1, grouped by line

A line that sits inside two independent report areas has more evidence against it than a line in one.

3 · Which lines hold the network together?from_node, to_node → graph

Build a graph: points are nodes, lines are edges. A line is a bridge if removing it splits the graph. When both sides still contain substations, losing it would separate parts of the mapped network, so a fault there matters more. Remember these are mapped connections only.

4 · How exposed was each line to the storm?storm polygon × segment geometry

Measure how many km of each line run inside the storm footprint. Long, fully exposed lines are more likely to have taken damage.

5 · Which line do we fly first?combines #1–#4

A starting score per line: evidence (each report's confidence split across its candidate lines by km), exposure (share of the line inside the storm) and importance (critical bridge = 1, other bridge = 0.4). It is a heuristic to tune, not a truth.

#LinekVkmScoreWhy
6 · Can we cover it in four hours?length_km × your drone specs

Compare what your fleet can inspect in the window with how much line there is. Transit to the first line and battery swaps eat into it. These drone numbers are placeholders; put in your own.

Recharging on the lines: in the scenario a sensor can only recharge from a line that is live and can supply enough power. LINE_024 is confirmed off, so it is ruled out. For every other line, being live is unknown until you check.
